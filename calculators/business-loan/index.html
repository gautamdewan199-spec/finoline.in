Structuring Commercial Credit: Term Loans vs. Working Capital CC/OD

Securing commercial debt is a vital milestone for scaling business operations, funding inventory purchases, or acquiring equipment. However, choosing the wrong debt structure can severely constrain corporate working capital. Using an interactive business loan calculator helps founders, finance teams, and MSME managers compare traditional term loan EMIs against daily-utilization Cash Credit (CC) and Overdraft (OD) interest models.

Term Loans vs. Cash Credit / Overdraft (CC/OD) Facilities

Understanding how commercial credit structures calculate interest is essential for liquidity management:

Structural Comparison Breakdown:

• Term Business Loans: Fixed principal disbursed upfront. Repaid in monthly EMIs (Principal + Interest) over 1 to 7 years. Best suited for capital expenditure (CapEx) such as machinery, factory setup, or technology infrastructure.

• Cash Credit / Overdraft (CC/OD): Revolving credit limit sanctioned against primary security (stock/receivables) or collateral. Interest is calculated on the actual daily closing balance utilized rather than the total limit, and billed monthly. Best suited for operating expenditure (OpEx) and bridging working capital gaps.

How to Optimize CC/OD Interest Outgo

Because CC/OD interest accrues on a daily basis:

  • Deposit Customer Receivables Immediately: Route daily business collections directly into your CC/OD account to lower the closing balance instantly, minimizing daily interest accrual.
  • Avoid Unutilized Commitment Charges: Ensure your sanctioned limit matches actual operating requirements. Banks frequently charge non-utilization fees if average limit usage drops below prescribed thresholds.
  • Maintain Stock & Debtors Statements: Submit monthly Stock and Receivables Audits (Drawing Power statements) on time to prevent accounts from going out-of-order, which triggers penal interest rates.

Key Underwriting Metrics Indian Banks Evaluate

When applying for commercial expansion credit, financial institutions assess debt serviceability via key financial ratios: Debt Service Coverage Ratio (DSCR) (preferring ratios above 1.25x–1.50x), Current Ratio (minimum 1.33x for working capital credit), and audited GST turnover records.
