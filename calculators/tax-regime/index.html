How to Choose Between the Old and New Income Tax Regime in India

Selecting between the Old Tax Regime and the New Tax Regime (Section 115BAC) is one of the highest-impact financial decisions salaried Indian taxpayers make each financial year. While the New Tax Regime serves as the default option with lower slab rates and an enhanced ₹75,000 standard deduction, it eliminates major wealth-building tax exemptions such as Section 80C (EPF, PPF, ELSS mutual funds), Section 80D (medical insurance), House Rent Allowance (HRA), and Section 24(b) home loan interest.

Use the break-even rule of thumb: if your total eligible deductions under the Old Regime are below ₹3.75 lakh on a ₹15 lakh+ salary, the New Tax Regime typically leaves more post-tax cash in your bank account. Conversely, if you service an active home loan and maximize Section 80C, 80D, and NPS Tier-I contributions, the Old Regime can still generate substantial annual tax savings.

Frequently Asked Questions (Income Tax Regime India)

1. Which is better: Old Tax Regime or New Tax Regime?

The New Tax Regime is better if your total deductions (80C, 80D, HRA, Home Loan interest) are low or if you prefer higher liquidity without locking money into tax-saving instruments. The Old Tax Regime wins only when your cumulative exemptions cross the break-even threshold—typically ₹3.75 lakh or more for middle-to-upper income brackets.

2. What is the standard deduction under the New Tax Regime vs. Old Tax Regime?

Salaried individuals and pensioners receive a flat standard deduction of ₹75,000 under the New Tax Regime, compared to ₹50,000 under the Old Tax Regime.

3. Can salaried employees switch between the Old and New Tax Regime every year?

Yes. Taxpayers with salary income (and no business or professional income) can freely switch between the Old and New Tax Regimes every financial year at the time of filing their Income Tax Return (ITR).

4. Are NPS employer contributions eligible under the New Tax Regime?

Yes. Under Section 80CCD(2), an employer’s contribution to your National Pension System (NPS) account up to 14% of basic salary (for private and government employees under the New Regime) remains deductible even when you opt for the New Tax Regime.
